This guide gives you the knowledge that makes confidence possible: what everything is, how it works, what to say, how to plan your workout, and a two-week plan that eases you in.

## Understanding Gym Anxiety

### Why it happens

Your brain is wired to feel anxious in unfamiliar social situations where you might be judged. A gym checks every box: new environment, unfamiliar equipment, and people who seem to know what they're doing.

### The truth about "everyone is watching"

Psychologists call it the **spotlight effect**: we consistently overestimate how much other people notice us. In reality, most people in the gym are focused on their own sets, their music and themselves in the mirror.

### Anxiety shrinks with exposure

Every time you go and nothing bad happens, your brain learns the gym is safe. Most women report their anxiety drops significantly after just **6–8 visits**. Your job is to get to visit #8.

> **The 2-minute rule:** On days when anxiety is high, commit to just walking in and doing one exercise. You can leave after that. You almost never will.

## Before Your First Visit

### Pick the right time

| Time | Busyness |
|---|---|
| 5–7 AM | Quiet. Regulars, focused crowd |
| 9 AM–3 PM (weekdays) | Quietest overall |
| 4–7 PM (weekdays) | Peak time |
| Weekend mornings | Busy |
| After 8 PM | Quiet |

Go during quiet hours for your first few visits.

### Take the tour

Most gyms offer a free orientation. Ask for one: "Hi, I'm new. Could someone show me where everything is?" Staff do this every day.

### What to wear

- Clothes you feel good in and can move freely in. Confidence matters more than brand.
- **Shoes:** flat, stable sneakers for lifting (or Converse-style shoes). Running shoes with thick cushions are less stable for squats and deadlifts.
- Hair tied back and away from your face.

### Have a plan before you walk in

Never walk in without knowing what you'll do. Open the 2-Week Plan, read today's workout and look up any exercise you don't know in the library before you arrive.

## The Gym Layout: Zones Explained

| Zone | What's there | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Cardio area | Treadmills, bikes, stair climbers, ellipticals | Warm-ups and cardio |
| Machine area | Selectorized machines (pin-loaded weight stacks) | Beginner-friendly strength training |
| Free-weight area | Dumbbells, benches, adjustable benches | Most of your strength training |
| Squat racks / platforms | Barbells, power racks, plates | Squats, deadlifts, hip thrusts |
| Cable area | Cable stations with adjustable pulleys | Kickbacks, rows, pushdowns, flyes |
| Stretching / turf area | Mats, foam rollers, bands, sleds | Warm-ups, core, stretching |

## Equipment 101

### Barbells

- **Standard Olympic barbell:** 45 lb (20 kg), 7 ft long.
- **Women's Olympic bar:** 33–35 lb (15 kg), thinner grip. Some gyms have these.
- **EZ curl bar & fixed barbells:** smaller pre-weighted bars, usually on a rack.

### Plates

Common plates are 2.5, 5, 10, 25, 35 and 45 lb. Always load the same weight on each side and **use collars (clips)** to keep plates from sliding.

### Pin-loaded machines

1. Adjust the seat and pads to fit your body (most have a pop pin or lever).
2. Insert the pin into the weight stack at your chosen weight.
3. Do your set with control. Don't let the stack slam.

### Cable machines

- **Pulley height:** pull the pin on the side column and slide it up or down.
- **Attachments:** handles, rope, straight bar, ankle strap. These are usually hanging on a rack nearby.
- **Weight:** the pin in the stack. Some cable machines have a 2:1 ratio, so the number on the stack doesn't match other machines. Just track your own numbers.

### Smith machine

A barbell on a fixed track. To unrack, rotate your wrists to unhook the bar from the slots; rotate back to rehook. It's great for beginners because the path is guided.

## Gym Etiquette: The Unwritten Rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Re-rack your weights where you found them | Leave plates on the bar or dumbbells on the floor |
| Wipe down equipment after use | Leave sweat on benches |
| Ask "how many sets do you have left?" | Take equipment someone is clearly using |
| Let people work in between your sets | Sit on a machine scrolling during long rests |
| Keep your stuff compact | Spread your bag, bottle and jacket across 3 machines |
| Stand back from the dumbbell rack after grabbing weights | Lift right in front of the rack, blocking others |
| Wear headphones | Play music from your speaker |
| Ask before filming near others | Film people without permission |

## Gym Scripts: What to Say

Knowing exactly what to say removes most of the stress. Save these:

**When you want a machine someone is using:**
> "Hey, how many sets do you have left?" or "Mind if I work in?"

**When you need a spot:**
> "Could you spot me for one set? It's 8 reps. Just help if I get stuck."

**When someone wants to work in with you:**
> "Sure! I've got two sets left, we can alternate."

**When you can't find something:**
> (To staff) "Where can I find the ankle straps / the hip thrust pad?"

**When someone gives unsolicited advice:**
> "Thanks! I'm following my coach's program." Then return to your set.

**When someone keeps talking and you want to train:**
> Point to your headphones, smile: "Sorry, I'm in the zone!"

**When someone is making you uncomfortable:**
> You don't owe anyone conversation. Move away, and **report it to staff**. Gyms take harassment seriously.

## How a Workout Is Structured

Every good workout follows a similar flow:

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. General warm-up | 5 min | Light cardio until you're warm |
| 2. Activation & ramp-up | 5 min | Bands, bridges, lighter sets of your first exercise |
| 3. Main lifts | 20–30 min | Biggest, hardest exercises first, when you're freshest |
| 4. Accessories | 15–20 min | Smaller exercises for specific muscles |
| 5. Finisher / core (optional) | 5 min | High-rep pump work or core |
| 6. Cooldown | 5 min | Easy walk and stretch |

## Reading a Program Like a Pro

| Notation | What it means |
|---|---|
| **3 × 12** | 3 sets of 12 reps |
| **3 × 10–12** | 3 sets of 10–12 reps. Use double progression (add weight once you hit 12 on all sets) |
| **/side** | Do the reps on each side |
| **RIR 2** | Stop when you could do about 2 more reps |
| **Rest 90s** | Rest 90 seconds between sets |
| **AMRAP** | As Many Reps As Possible (with good form) |
| **Superset** | Two exercises back to back with no rest between them |
| **Tempo 3-1-1** | 3 sec lowering, 1 sec pause, 1 sec lifting |
| **Drop set** | Finish a set, immediately reduce weight ~20–30% and continue |

::quiz
Q: What does "3 × 10–12, RIR 2" mean?
- 3 reps of 10–12 sets
* 3 sets of 10–12 reps, stopping about 2 reps before failure
- Rest 2 minutes
E: Sets × reps, and RIR is how many good reps you have left in the tank at the end of the set.
::

## Choosing Weights as a Beginner

The right weight lets you finish all your reps with good form and feel challenged in the last 2–3 reps.

1. Start lighter than you think.
2. Do a set and ask: "Could I do 2–3 more good reps?"
3. If you could do 5+ more, go heavier next set. If you couldn't finish with good form, go lighter.
4. Write down the weight in your log. Next time, start there.

> **Remember:** Everyone in that gym started with light weights. The only way to lift heavy is to start where you are.

## Filming Your Workouts

Filming helps you check your form and creates content if you want it.

- Set your phone up **out of walkways** (on the floor, a tripod or a bench you're using).
- Film from the **side** for squats, hinges and presses, and from the **front** for knee tracking.
- Don't film other people without permission.
- Compare your videos to the technique cues in the Exercise Library.

## Safety & Boundaries

- Trust your instincts. If something or someone feels off, leave the area or tell staff.
- Most gyms have a code of conduct. Unwanted comments, touching or filming can be reported.
- Use the buddy system at night: walk out with a friend or ask staff to watch you to your car.
- Share your location with a friend if you train very early or late.

## Building the Habit

Confidence comes from consistency. These strategies help it stick:

- **Schedule it** like an appointment, with the same days and times each week.
- **Habit stack:** "After I drop the kids off, I drive to the gym." Link the gym to an existing routine.
- **Pack your bag the night before** and leave it by the door.
- **Minimum viable workout:** on hard days, do just the first 2 exercises. Showing up keeps the habit alive.
- **Track it:** check off every workout. Seeing your streak grow is powerful.
