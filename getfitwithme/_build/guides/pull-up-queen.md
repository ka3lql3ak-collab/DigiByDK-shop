## Welcome & How This Program Works

Can't do a single pull-up yet? Good. That's exactly who I wrote this for. A pull-up is one of the hardest bodyweight moves there is, and it's one of the most satisfying things you'll ever do in the gym. Over the next 8 weeks you'll build the exact strength it takes to pull your chin over that bar. Then you'll test it, film it and keep stacking reps.

### The structure

| Day | Session | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Pull Strength | Heavy assisted pull-ups, slow negatives, rows, dead hangs |
| Day 2 | Lats + Core | Lat pulldowns, inverted rows, straight-arm pulldowns, hollow holds |
| Day 3 | Skill + Volume | Band-assisted pull-ups, flexed-arm hangs, cable rows, face pulls |

Train on three non-consecutive days, such as Monday, Wednesday and Friday. You can run it on its own or alongside lower-body training. If you're also running the Glute Specialist Program, swap that program's Day 2 (Upper Body) for these three days.

### The phases

| Weeks | Phase | Main sets × reps | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Foundation | 3 × 8 | Learn the movement, find your starting assistance |
| 3–4 | Build | 4 × 6 | Less assistance, longer negatives |
| 5 | Deload | 2 × 6 | Half the work. Your body catches up |
| 6 | Strength | 5 × 4 | Lowest assistance yet, first unassisted attempt |
| 7 | Peak | 4 × 3 | Minimum help, daily pull-up singles |
| 8 | Pull-Up Day | Test | Your first real pull-up (or your new max) |

> **Coach's note:** A pull-up isn't about being skinny or "naturally strong." It's a skill plus a strength. You practice the skill three days a week, and you build the strength one less pound of assistance at a time.

::quiz
Q: What happens in Week 5?
- You test your max
* A deload: half the sets so your strength can catch up
- You add extra sets
E: Week 5 is a planned recovery week. Strength shows up AFTER recovery, which is why Week 6 is your strongest week yet.
::

## Test Day: Find Your Starting Point

Before Week 1, spend one session finding out where you are. Write down every number. You'll compare against them in Week 8.

### The 4 tests

| Test | How | Write down |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Dead hang | Hang from the bar with straight arms as long as you can | Seconds |
| 2. Flexed-arm hang | Step up so your chin is over the bar, then hold | Seconds |
| 3. Negative | Lower from the top as slowly as you can | Seconds to the bottom |
| 4. Pull-up | One honest attempt from a dead hang | Reps (0 is a fine answer) |

Rest 3 minutes between tests.

### Where to start

| Your results | Your level | Start here |
|---|---|---|
| Hang under 20 sec, no flexed hang | Level 1 | Heavy assistance. Hold the shorter times in Week 1 with toes on the box if needed |
| Hang 20–45 sec, flexed hang under 10 sec | Level 2 | Follow the program exactly as written |
| Flexed hang 10+ sec, negative 5+ sec | Level 3 | Use less assistance from Week 1 and the longer negatives |
| 1–3 real pull-ups already | Level 4 | Replace the assisted pull-ups with real reps and use the band only for the last sets |

### Finding your assistance weight

On the assisted pull-up machine, **more weight means more help**. Start at about half your body weight. Do 8 reps. If the last 2 reps were hard but clean, that's your Week 1 number. If you blew through 8, take off 10 lb. If you couldn't finish 8 clean reps, add 10 lb.

::quiz
Q: On an assisted pull-up machine, what does adding more weight do?
* Gives you more help, so the exercise gets easier
- Makes the exercise harder
- Nothing, it's just for balance
E: The weight is a counterbalance that pushes you up. Your goal every week is to need LESS of it.
::

## Why Pull-Ups Feel Impossible (and Why They Aren't)

If you've ever hung from a bar and gone absolutely nowhere, you're in very good company. Most women can't do a pull-up the first time they try. Here's why, and why it changes fast.

### The real reasons

1. **Nobody trained it.** Most women's programs are full of glutes and legs and almost no vertical pulling. Strength comes from practice, and you never practiced.
2. **The strength curve.** The hardest part of a pull-up is the very first inch from a dead hang. Most people never build strength there, so they never get started.
3. **Grip gives out first.** Many women are strong enough to pull but can't hold on long enough. Dead hangs fix this.
4. **Relative strength.** A pull-up means lifting your whole body. Getting stronger matters far more than losing weight, but both help.

### Why this program works

You'll train the pull-up from every angle:
- **Assisted reps** train the full movement with less load.
- **Negatives** build strength in the lowering phase, where muscles are about 20–40% stronger. Your lats learn to control your full body weight long before they can lift it.
- **Holds** build strength at the top and in the middle, where most attempts fail.
- **Rows and pulldowns** grow the lats and mid-back muscles that do the work.

> **Truth bomb:** You don't "find out" if you can do a pull-up. You build it. Every rep in this program is a brick.

## Pull-Up Anatomy

### Lats (latissimus dorsi)

Your biggest back muscles. They run from under your armpits to your lower back and pull your elbows down to your sides. They're the main engine of a pull-up, and strong lats are also what create that snatched-waist V-shape.

### Mid-back (rhomboids, middle and lower traps)

These pull your shoulder blades down and together. The very first movement of every pull-up starts here. That's why you'll do scapular pull-ups.

### Biceps and forearms

Your biceps help bend the elbow, especially near the top. Your forearms hold you on the bar. Hammer curls and dead hangs train both.

### Core

A tight core stops your body from swinging and makes the pull more efficient. Hollow body holds and hanging knee raises teach you to stay in one piece on the bar.

::quiz
Q: Which muscle is the main engine of a pull-up?
- Biceps
* Lats
- Chest
E: Your lats pull your elbows down to your sides. Biceps help, but the lats do most of the work.
::

## Perfect Pull-Up Technique

### The setup

1. **Grip:** palms facing away, hands slightly wider than your shoulders, thumbs wrapped around the bar.
2. **Dead hang:** arms straight, legs together, a slight squeeze in your glutes and abs.
3. **Body:** a very slight "hollow" shape, with ribs down and legs slightly in front of you. Not a big arch, not a big swing.

### The rep, step by step

1. **Set the shoulders.** Pull your shoulder blades DOWN, away from your ears. Your body rises an inch and your arms stay straight. This is the scapular pull-up you'll practice.
2. **Drive the elbows.** Think about pulling your elbows down into your back pockets, not about pulling with your hands.
3. **Chest to the bar.** Lift your chest toward the bar. Your chin clears the bar naturally.
4. **Pause.** Hold the top for one second. Squeeze.
5. **Lower with control.** Two to three seconds back to straight arms. Every rep starts from a full hang.

### Pull-up vs chin-up

| | Pull-up | Chin-up |
|---|---|---|
| Grip | Palms away, slightly wide | Palms toward you, shoulder-width |
| Main muscles | Lats, mid-back | Lats plus more biceps |
| Difficulty | Harder | Usually easier for beginners |

Many women get a chin-up first. That's a huge win, so test both in Week 8.

### Common mistakes

- **Kicking and swinging (kipping).** It looks like a pull-up but builds none of the strength.
- **Half reps.** If your arms don't straighten at the bottom, the rep doesn't count.
- **Chin poking.** Reaching your chin up instead of bringing your chest to the bar.
- **Shrugging.** Shoulders up by your ears makes the lift harder and strains your neck.
- **Dropping.** Falling out of the top wastes the best strength-building part of the rep.

::quiz
Q: What should you think about to pull with your lats, not just your arms?
- Squeeze the bar as hard as possible
* Drive your elbows down toward your back pockets
- Pull your chin up first
E: "Elbows to back pockets" switches on your lats. Your hands are just hooks.
::

## The Pull-Up Ladder: Assistance, Negatives & Holds

Think of your first pull-up as the top of a ladder. Each tool below is a step.

### Step 1: Assisted pull-ups (machine or band)

Your main strength builder. You do the full movement with help, then you need less help every week.

**Machine vs band:** The machine gives the same help through the whole rep and is easy to track in pounds. A band gives the MOST help at the bottom (where it's stretched) and the least at the top. Use the machine on Day 1 for strength and the band on Day 3 for skill.

| Band color (typical) | Help |
|---|---|
| Red (thin) | ~10–35 lb |
| Black | ~25–65 lb |
| Purple | ~35–85 lb |
| Green (thick) | ~50–120 lb |

Colors vary by brand. Just remember: thinner band, less help.

### Step 2: Negatives

Jump or step to the top and lower yourself as slowly as you can. Negatives build strength faster than almost anything else, but they also cause the most soreness. That's why they're limited to Day 1 and kept to a few quality reps.

**Rule:** if your lowering speeds up or you drop through the bottom half, the set is over.

### Step 3: Holds

Flexed-arm hangs build strength at the top. Dead hangs build grip and healthy shoulders. Hollow holds teach you to stay tight. If you can hold your chin over the bar for 30 seconds, a pull-up is very close.

### Step 4: Real reps

From Week 6, you'll try one unassisted pull-up at the start of training while you're fresh. Some women get it in Week 6. Some in Week 8. Some after a second round. All of them get it.

::quiz
Q: When should a negative set end?
- After exactly 10 reps
* When your lowering speeds up or you drop through the bottom
- Never, keep going until you can't hang on
E: Slow, controlled lowering is what builds strength. Once it turns into dropping, you're only adding soreness.
::

## How to Progress Every Week

### The assistance rule

Each week, if every rep last week was clean and controlled, **take 5 lb off the machine** (or 5–10 lb if you're still above half your body weight). If you missed reps, keep the same assistance and beat it next week.

### Change ONE thing at a time

In any week you can make it harder in one of these ways. Pick one:
- Less assistance
- A longer negative (one more second)
- A longer hold (5 more seconds)
- One more rep on rows or pulldowns

Changing everything at once is how progress stalls.

### Track it

Log every set in the Program tab. For assisted pull-ups, log the assistance weight in the weight box. A smaller number is better. Your Progress tab charts the lifts where heavier is better: lat pulldown, rows and straight-arm pulldowns.

### Reading your effort (RIR)

| RIR | Feels like |
|---|---|
| 3 | Challenging, reps still fast |
| 2 | Hard. Reps slow down |
| 1 | Very hard. One clean rep left |
| 0 | You couldn't do another |

Stay at RIR 1–2 on the main pulls. Pull-ups are a skill, and practicing sloppy reps teaches sloppy reps.

::quiz
Q: Last week every assisted pull-up was clean. What do you do this week?
* Take about 5 lb of assistance off the machine
- Add 5 lb of assistance
- Keep everything exactly the same forever
E: Less assistance means more of YOU doing the lifting. That's the whole program.
::

## Grip, Hands & Shoulder Health

### Grip

If your hands give out before your back does, your grip is the weak link.
- Do every dead hang in the program, and don't skip them when you're tired.
- Chalk helps a lot. Liquid chalk works in gyms that ban the powder.
- Wrap your thumbs around the bar. A thumbless grip slips.

### Calluses

Calluses are normal. Torn calluses aren't. File them down weekly with a pumice stone, keep your hands moisturized, and hold the bar at the base of your fingers rather than deep in your palm.

### Shoulder warm-up (5 minutes, every session)

1. Arm circles, 10 forward and 10 back
2. Band pull-aparts × 15
3. Scapular pull-ups × 8
4. Dead hang × 15 seconds
5. One light set of your first exercise

### The rules

- Sharp pain means stop. Muscle burn is fine. Joint pain isn't.
- Elbow pain often comes from gripping too narrow or going too hard on negatives. Widen slightly and reduce negatives for a week.
- Never drop from the top of a pull-up onto straight arms with loose shoulders.

## Body Weight, Nutrition & Recovery

### Does body weight matter?

Yes and no. A pull-up means lifting your body weight, so carrying a little less makes it easier. But **getting stronger matters far more than getting smaller**. Many women hit their first pull-up while maintaining or even gaining weight, because they built muscle.

Don't crash diet for a pull-up. A big calorie deficit makes it very hard to build strength.

### Fuel for strength

- **Protein:** 0.7–1.0 g per lb of goal body weight daily.
- **Calories:** maintenance or a small deficit (no more than about 300 calories).
- **Before training:** a carb + protein meal 1–3 hours before.
- **Creatine monohydrate:** 3–5 g daily is one of the best-researched supplements for strength.

See **Fuel Like A Baddie** for full macro calculations and meal plans.

### Recovery

Sleep is when your back actually gets stronger. Aim for 7–9 hours. Keep at least one rest day between pull-up sessions, and take the Week 5 deload seriously. It's part of the program, not a break from it.

## Pull-Up Day Protocol

Week 8, Day 1. This is the day you've been building toward. Make it an event.

### Before Pull-Up Day

- Sleep well for 2 nights before.
- Eat a solid meal 2–3 hours before training.
- Set up your phone to film from the side.
- Bring chalk, and your hype playlist.

### The warm-up

| Step | What | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shoulder warm-up from Chapter 8 | — |
| 2 | Assisted pull-ups × 5 at your Week 1 assistance | 2 min |
| 3 | Assisted pull-ups × 2 at your lowest assistance | 2 min |
| 4 | Flexed-arm hang × 5 seconds | 3 min |

### The attempt

1. Chalk up. Grip slightly wider than your shoulders.
2. Dead hang, ribs down, legs together.
3. Shoulders DOWN first.
4. Elbows to your back pockets. Chest to the bar.
5. Don't stop pulling until your chin clears.

You get **3 attempts**, with 3–4 minutes of rest between them. If the pull-up goes, try for a second rep on the next attempt. If you got one rep, try a chin-up too.

### Didn't get it yet?

You're closer than you think. Compare your Test Day numbers: your hang time, flexed-arm hang and negative time will all be dramatically better. Take an easy week, then rerun Weeks 3–8 starting from your current assistance. Most women who don't get it in round one get it in round two.

### Log it

Enter your result on the **PR Wall**, compare it with your Test Day, and tag me in the video. I want to see it!

## Frequently Asked Questions

**I can't hold on to the bar for even 10 seconds. Where do I start?**
Level 1 in Chapter 2. Do the dead hangs with your toes lightly on a box, use heavy assistance and follow the plan. Grip comes back fast.

**My gym doesn't have an assisted pull-up machine.**
Use a band for all assisted pull-ups, and use the lat pulldown to track strength in pounds.

**Can I do this at home?**
You'll need a doorway or mounted pull-up bar, a set of loop bands and a few dumbbells. Replace the cable exercises with band rows and band straight-arm pulldowns.

**Will pull-ups make my back bulky?**
No. They'll build a stronger, more defined back and shoulders, which makes your waist look smaller. Building big muscle takes years of trying on purpose.

**I'm sore for days after negatives.**
Normal at the start. Do fewer reps, keep the full slow lowering, and the soreness drops after 2–3 weeks.

**What do I do after Week 8?**
If you got your pull-up: rerun Weeks 3–8 using real reps for your first sets and the band only after that. If you didn't yet: rerun Weeks 3–8 from your current assistance. Either way, you're stronger than you were 8 weeks ago.
