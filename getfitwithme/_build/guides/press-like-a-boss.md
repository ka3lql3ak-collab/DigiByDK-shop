## Welcome & How This Program Works

Y'all saw me press 70s and 75s. This is the program that got me there. It's a 6-week upper-body strength block built around one goal: **getting your dumbbell press as strong as possible**, while building sculpted shoulders, a strong back and toned arms along the way.

### The structure

| Day | Session | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Heavy Push | Heavy flat & incline pressing, shoulders, triceps |
| Day 2 | Pull + Posture | Lats, mid-back, rear delts, biceps |
| Day 3 | Volume Push | Paused presses, chest isolation, shoulders, triceps |

Run it on its own (3 days/week), or pair it with lower-body days for a 5–6 day split. If you're also running the Glute Specialist Program, replace that program's Day 2 (Upper Body) with these three days.

### The phases

| Weeks | Phase | Main lift sets × reps | Target RIR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Foundation | 3 × 10 | 2–3 |
| 3–4 | Build | 4 × 8 | 1–2 |
| 5 | Strength | 5 × 5 | 1–2 |
| 6 | PR Week | Work up to a heavy 3 | 1 |

> **Coach's note:** Strength is a skill. Pressing the same movement three times a week with perfect technique is how you get strong fast. Don't swap the main lifts. Own them.

## Upper-Body Anatomy for Women Who Lift

### Chest (pectoralis major)

Your pecs push your arms forward and across your body. The **upper (clavicular) chest** is trained more by incline pressing. The **mid/lower chest** is trained by flat pressing and flyes. A developed chest doesn't make you "bulky". It lifts and shapes your upper body and supports your posture.

### Shoulders (deltoids)

Three heads: **front** (pressing), **side** (lateral raises, which give the "capped" look and make your waist look smaller), and **rear** (face pulls, rear delt flyes, which pull your shoulders back for better posture).

### Triceps

Two-thirds of your upper arm. Strong triceps help your press and tone the back of the arm. The **long head** is trained best with your arm overhead (overhead extensions).

### Back (lats, rhomboids, traps)

Your back is the base you press from. A strong upper back keeps your shoulder blades stable on the bench, which makes you stronger and protects your shoulders. Your lats also create the V-taper that makes your waist look smaller.

> **The balance rule:** For every pressing set, do roughly one pulling set. This program keeps that balance, which is why Day 2 is not optional.

## Dumbbell Press Masterclass

The dumbbell press is the backbone of this program. Here's how to do it like a pro.

### Getting heavy dumbbells into position (the kick-up)

1. Sit on the end of the bench with the dumbbells standing upright on your thighs, close to your knees.
2. Grip them firmly and take a breath.
3. Lie back in one smooth motion, and as you roll back, drive one knee up into one dumbbell, then the other (or both at once), popping them to your chest.
4. Your momentum carries the dumbbells into the start position.

### The setup

- **Shoulder blades:** pinch them together and pull them down, like tucking them into your back pockets. Keep them there for every rep.
- **Arch:** a natural arch in your lower back is safe and helps you press. Your glutes stay on the bench.
- **Feet:** planted flat, pushing into the floor (leg drive).
- **Wrists:** stacked over your elbows, knuckles toward the ceiling.

### The rep

1. Start with the dumbbells over your shoulders.
2. Lower them toward your lower chest over about 2 seconds, with your elbows about **45°** from your torso.
3. At the bottom, the dumbbells are roughly level with your chest and your forearms are vertical.
4. Press up and slightly in, so the dumbbells finish above your shoulders. They don't need to touch.

### Getting out of the set safely

Bring the dumbbells to your chest, tuck your knees up and rock forward to sit up with the dumbbells resting on your thighs. **Never drop heavy dumbbells out to the sides.** It's dangerous for your shoulders and annoying for everyone around you.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Elbows flared to 90° | Tuck to 45°. It's stronger and kinder to your shoulders |
| Shoulders rolling forward at the top | Keep shoulder blades pinned. Stop the press just short of reaching |
| Bouncing at the bottom | Control the lowering, brief pause, then press |
| Feet floating | Plant your feet and push them into the floor |
| Dumbbells drifting toward your face | Press over your shoulders, lower to your lower chest |

::quiz
Q: What elbow angle should you use on dumbbell presses?
- 90°, straight out to the sides
* About 45° from your torso
- Elbows touching your ribs
E: About 45° is strong and shoulder-friendly. Flaring to 90° puts more stress on the front of the shoulder.
::

## The Dumbbell Jump Problem (and How to Beat It)

Here's the challenge nobody talks about: dumbbells usually go up in 5 lb increments **per hand**. Going from 25s to 30s is a **20% jump**, which is huge. This is where most women get stuck.

### Solution 1: Double progression with a wide rep range

Stay with a weight until you can do **every set at the top of the rep range**, then move up. The extra reps build the strength you need to handle the jump.

### Solution 2: The "one set heavier" bridge

When you're ready to jump weights but can't hit all your sets:

| Session | Set 1 | Sets 2–4 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 30s × 6 | 25s × 10 |
| B | 30s × 7 | 25s × 10 |
| C | 30s × 8, 30s × 6 | 25s × 10 |
| D | All sets with 30s ✅ | |

### Solution 3: Slow it down

Add a 3-second lowering phase or a 2-second pause. The same weight becomes much harder, so you build strength without needing a heavier dumbbell.

### Solution 4: Microloading

Some gyms have 2.5 lb dumbbell increments or magnetic add-on weights. If yours does, use them.

> **Coach's note:** It took me months to get from the 50s to the 70s. Every 5 lb jump is a real achievement. Celebrate each one and log it on your PR Wall.

::quiz
Q: Going from 25 lb to 30 lb dumbbells is roughly what percentage jump?
- 5%
- 10%
* 20%
E: 5 ÷ 25 = 20%. That's why wide rep ranges and the 'one set heavier' bridge matter.
::

## Shoulder Health & Injury Prevention

Pressing heavy is safe when you do it right. These habits keep your shoulders healthy for years.

### Warm-up for every push day (5 minutes)

1. Band pull-aparts × 15
2. Band external rotations × 12 each arm
3. Arm circles forward and back × 10
4. Push-ups × 8–10 (on knees or incline is fine)
5. Ramp-up sets on your first press (see the Warm-Up Calculator in Tools)

### The rules

- **Pull as much as you push.** Face pulls and rows protect your shoulders.
- **Don't press through pain.** Sharp or pinching pain in the front of the shoulder means stop. Try a neutral grip (palms facing each other), a lower range, or a machine press instead.
- **Control the bottom.** Shoulders are most vulnerable at the deepest point. Never bounce there.

### Neutral-grip pressing

If regular dumbbell presses bother your shoulders, try pressing with your palms facing each other. Most people can press pain-free this way, and it's just as effective for the chest and triceps.

## Training Your Back for a Bigger Press

A strong back gives you a stable platform to press from, and it creates the posture and V-taper that makes your waist look snatched.

### Key principles

- **Pull with your elbows, not your hands.** Imagine your hands are hooks.
- **Full stretch, full squeeze.** Let your shoulder blades move. Reach at the bottom of a row, squeeze at the top.
- **Control the lowering.** Two to three seconds on every rep.

### Back exercise effectiveness

| Exercise | Main target | Why it's in the program |
|---|---|---|
| Lat Pulldown | Lats | Width and V-taper, shoulder health |
| Single-Arm DB Row | Lats, mid-back | Fixes left/right imbalances |
| Face Pulls | Rear delts, rotator cuff | Posture and shoulder health |
| Rear Delt Fly | Rear delts | Balances all the pressing |

## Spotting, Safety & Gym Confidence

### When you need a spotter

During Weeks 5 and 6, your sets are heavy enough that a spotter helps. With dumbbells, the spotter should support your **wrists or forearms**, never your elbows.

### How to ask for a spot

> "Hey, could you spot me for one set? It's [number] reps. Just help at the wrists if I get stuck."

Most regulars at the gym are happy to help. Asking is a sign that you take your training seriously.

### If you get stuck without a spotter

Lower the dumbbells to your chest, bring your knees up, and roll up to sitting with the dumbbells on your thighs. Practice this with light weights so it's automatic.

::quiz
Q: Where should a spotter help on a dumbbell press?
- At your elbows
* At your wrists/forearms
- By pushing your chest
E: Spotting at the wrists or forearms controls the dumbbells directly.
::

## Reading Your Effort (RIR)

RIR = Reps In Reserve: how many good reps you could still do when you stop.

| RIR | Feels like |
|---|---|
| 3 | Challenging but fast |
| 2 | Hard. Reps slow down |
| 1 | Very hard. One rep left |
| 0 | Failure. Only on machines and isolation moves |

On presses, stay at **RIR 1–2**. Missing a dumbbell press rep is how injuries happen. Save failure for cable flyes, pushdowns and lateral raises.

## PR Week Protocol

Week 6 is where you show off everything you've built. Here's how to test safely on Day 1.

### Before PR day

- Sleep well for 2 nights before.
- Eat a solid carb + protein meal 2–3 hours before training.
- Bring a spotter or film the set.

### The warm-up ladder

Use the Warm-Up Calculator in the Tools tab, or follow this pattern based on your target weight:

| Set | Weight | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~40% | 10 | 1 min |
| 2 | ~60% | 5 | 1.5 min |
| 3 | ~75% | 3 | 2 min |
| 4 | ~85% | 2 | 2–3 min |
| 5 | ~92% | 1 | 3 min |
| 6 | **Target heavy 3** | 3 | 3–4 min |
| 7 (optional) | +5 lb per DB | 1–3 | — |

Stop when the next set wouldn't be clean. A slow, grindy rep is fine. A rep where your form falls apart is not.

### Log it

Enter your result on the **PR Wall**. The app calculates your estimated 1-rep max using the Epley formula: **weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)**.

## Nutrition for Strength

- **Protein:** 0.7–1.0 g per lb of goal body weight daily.
- **Calories:** at least maintenance during this program. Strength is hard to build in a big deficit.
- **Carbs before training:** a carb + protein meal 1–3 hours before you press.
- **Creatine monohydrate:** 3–5 g daily is one of the best-researched supplements for strength.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will lifting heavy make my arms bulky?**
No. Women build upper-body muscle slowly, partly because of lower testosterone. What you'll see is tighter, more defined shoulders and arms.

**Can I use a barbell bench press instead?**
Yes, the principles are the same, but dumbbells are easier to learn, safer without a spotter and fix strength imbalances. That's why I use them.

**My left side is weaker than my right.**
That's normal. Dumbbells will even it out over time. Let the weaker side set the reps.

**How often should I test my max?**
Once per training block (every 6–8 weeks). Testing too often takes away from training.

**What do I do after Week 6?**
Take an easy week, then rerun the program starting with weights about 5–10% heavier than your Week 1 weights.
