## Welcome: Why Nutrition Changes Everything

You can train perfectly and still not see results if your nutrition isn't aligned with your goal. Food decides whether you build muscle, lose fat or stay exactly where you are.

This guide replaces guessing with a system:

1. **Calculate** your personal calories and macros (the Macro Calculator tab).
2. **Learn** the few principles that actually matter (this guide).
3. **Follow** the 7-day high-protein meal plan and recipes, or build your own meals with the plate method.
4. **Shop** with the grocery checklist and **prep** once a week.
5. **Adjust** every 2 weeks based on your progress.

> **My promise:** No starving. No cutting out entire food groups. No detox teas. Just real food, enough protein and a plan you can follow on a busy week.

## Energy Balance: The Foundation

Your body weight is ultimately driven by **energy balance**: the calories you eat compared with the calories you burn.

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| Eat **less** than you burn (deficit) | You lose weight |
| Eat about **the same** (maintenance) | Your weight stays stable |
| Eat **more** than you burn (surplus) | You gain weight |

### What makes up the calories you burn (TDEE)

| Component | Share of daily burn | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| BMR (basal metabolic rate) | ~60–70% | Keeping you alive at rest |
| NEAT (non-exercise activity) | ~15–30% | Steps, chores, fidgeting, standing |
| Exercise | ~5–10% | Your workouts |
| TEF (thermic effect of food) | ~10% | Digesting food (protein burns the most) |

> **The surprise:** Your daily steps often burn more than your workouts. This is why I make you hit your steps.

### Why weight and fat aren't the same thing

Whether the weight you lose or gain is **fat or muscle** depends on your **protein intake** and **strength training**. Eating enough protein and lifting while you're in a deficit is how you lose fat and keep your shape.

::quiz
Q: What usually burns more calories per day for most people: workouts or daily steps and movement (NEAT)?
- Workouts, always
* Daily movement (NEAT)
- Neither burns calories
E: NEAT (steps, chores, standing) often accounts for 15–30% of daily burn, more than a typical workout.
::

## Macros Explained

Calories come from three macronutrients:

| Macro | Calories per gram | Main job | Daily target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | 4 | Builds and repairs muscle, keeps you full | 0.7–1.0 g per lb of goal weight |
| **Carbohydrates** | 4 | Fuel for training, brain and mood | The rest of your calories |
| **Fat** | 9 | Hormones, vitamin absorption, flavor | ~25–30% of calories (min ~0.3 g/lb) |

### Protein: your #1 priority

Protein is the most important macro for women who train:

- It provides the building blocks for muscle.
- It's the most filling macro, which makes a deficit easier.
- It has the highest thermic effect (about 20–30% of its calories are burned during digestion).

### Carbs aren't the enemy

Carbs fuel hard training and recovery. Women who cut carbs too low often feel flat in the gym, crave sweets and lose strength. Prioritize whole-food carbs (rice, potatoes, oats, fruit, beans, whole grains) and time them around your workouts.

### Fat: don't go too low

Dietary fat supports hormone production, including those that regulate your menstrual cycle. Don't let fat drop below about 0.3 g per lb of body weight for long periods.

::quiz
Q: How many calories are in one gram of fat?
- 4
- 7
* 9
E: Fat has 9 calories per gram. Protein and carbs have 4, and alcohol has 7.
::

## Using Your Macro Calculator

The **My Macros** tab uses the **Mifflin-St Jeor equation**, one of the most accurate formulas for estimating calorie needs, combined with an activity multiplier.

### Choosing your activity level

| Option | Choose this if... |
|---|---|
| Lift 2–3×/week | Mostly sitting during the day, under ~7,000 steps |
| Lift 4–5×/week | 7,000–10,000 steps, lifting regularly |
| Daily training / active job | On your feet all day or 12,000+ steps |

### Choosing your goal

- **Lose fat:** −400 kcal from maintenance. Expect about 0.5–1 lb per week.
- **Maintain & tone (recomp):** best for newer lifters and anyone who wants to build shape without the scale moving much.
- **Build muscle:** +250 kcal. Slow, lean gains.

### The 2-week adjustment rule

Your calculator numbers are a **starting point**, not a final answer. Everyone's metabolism is slightly different.

1. Follow your numbers consistently for 2 weeks.
2. Weigh yourself 3–7 mornings per week (after the bathroom, before eating) and compare **weekly averages**.
3. Adjust:

| Goal | If your weekly average... | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Lose fat | Hasn't dropped in 2 weeks | Reduce by 100–150 kcal (from carbs or fat) or add 2,000 daily steps |
| Lose fat | Is dropping more than 1% of body weight/week | Add 100–150 kcal |
| Build | Hasn't moved up in 2–3 weeks | Add 100–150 kcal |
| Build | Is rising more than 0.5 lb/week | Reduce by 100 kcal |

::quiz
Q: You're trying to lose fat and your weekly average weight hasn't moved in 2 weeks. What's the first adjustment?
- Cut calories in half
* Reduce by 100–150 kcal or add ~2,000 daily steps
- Stop eating carbs completely
E: Small adjustments keep the process sustainable. Big cuts backfire through hunger, fatigue and muscle loss.
::

## The Plate Method (No Tracking Required)

Not everyone wants to log every bite. The plate method gets you about 80% of the results with no apps.

### Build your plate with your hand

| Component | Portion per meal (women) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 1–2 palms | Chicken, fish, lean beef, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu |
| Carbs | 1–2 cupped hands | Rice, potatoes, oats, pasta, fruit, beans |
| Veggies | 1–2 fists | Broccoli, salad, peppers, green beans |
| Fats | 1 thumb | Oil, butter, avocado, cheese, nuts |

### Adjust for your goal

- **Fat loss:** 1 cupped hand of carbs at most meals, 2 palms of protein, extra veggies.
- **Build:** 2 cupped hands of carbs, especially around training.
- **Training days:** more carbs at the meals before and after your workout.

## Meal Timing & Workout Nutrition

Total daily intake matters most. Timing is the next level.

### Before training (1–3 hours)

A meal with **protein + carbs**, moderate fat and lower fiber.
- Chicken + rice + a little veggies
- Greek yogurt + granola + berries
- Bagel with eggs
- If you train early: banana + protein shake 30–60 min before

### After training (within a few hours)

Another **protein + carbs** meal. There's no urgent 30-minute "anabolic window", but eating within a few hours helps recovery.

### Protein distribution

Spread protein over **3–5 meals** with 25–40 g each, rather than eating most of it at dinner.

## Hydration & Electrolytes

- **Baseline:** about half your body weight (lb) in ounces of water daily, plus 12–24 oz per hour of training.
- **Check your urine:** pale yellow is the goal.
- **Electrolytes:** if you sweat a lot, train in heat or feel lightheaded, add salt to meals or use an electrolyte mix.
- **Caffeine counts** toward hydration in moderation, and it doesn't dehydrate habitual coffee drinkers.

## Fiber, Micronutrients & Gut Health

- **Fiber:** aim for 25–35 g per day from vegetables, fruit, beans, oats and whole grains. It keeps you full and supports digestion.
- **Eat the rainbow:** different colored fruits and vegetables provide different vitamins and antioxidants.
- **Iron:** women who menstruate need more. Get it from red meat, beans, spinach and fortified cereals, paired with vitamin C. If you're constantly exhausted, ask your doctor to check your iron.
- **Calcium & vitamin D:** important for bone health in women who lift. Dairy, fortified alternatives, leafy greens and sunlight.

## How to Read a Nutrition Label

1. **Serving size first.** Everything on the label refers to one serving, and many packages contain several.
2. **Calories** per serving.
3. **Protein**: anything with 10 g+ protein per 100 calories is an excellent protein source.
4. **Added sugars**: try to keep them under ~25 g per day.
5. **Fiber**: more is better.
6. **Ingredients** are listed by weight, so the first ingredient is the main one.

> **The protein-per-calorie trick:** Divide grams of protein by calories × 100. A protein bar with 20 g protein and 200 calories = 10 g per 100 kcal (great). One with 10 g and 250 calories = 4 g per 100 kcal (basically a candy bar).

::quiz
Q: A bar has 12 g protein and 240 calories. How many grams of protein per 100 calories is that?
* 5 g
- 12 g
- 20 g
E: 12 ÷ 240 × 100 = 5 g per 100 kcal. That's a snack, not a protein source. Aim for 10 g+ per 100 kcal.
::

## Cravings, Weekends & Alcohol

### The 80/20 approach

Eat whole, minimally processed foods about 80% of the time and leave 20% for foods you love. Restriction that's too strict leads to binges, while flexibility keeps you consistent.

### Handling cravings

- Eat enough protein and fiber. Most cravings are worse when you're under-eating.
- Have your treat **after a meal**, not on an empty stomach.
- Keep high-protein sweet options ready (protein pudding, Greek yogurt with honey, protein mug cake).
- Don't keep trigger foods in the house in large amounts.

### Weekends

A common pattern is being "perfect" Monday to Friday and then eating 1,000+ extra calories each weekend day, which completely wipes out the weekly deficit. Keep weekends flexible but anchored: protein at every meal, plan your fun meal, and stay active.

### Alcohol

Alcohol has 7 calories per gram, reduces sleep quality and recovery, and lowers your inhibitions around food. If you drink:
- Choose lower-calorie options (vodka soda, light beer, wine spritzer).
- Alternate every drink with water.
- Eat a protein-rich meal beforehand.
- Keep it to 1–2 drinks.

| ✅ Proven | Protein powder | A convenient way to hit protein |

> Check with your doctor before starting supplements, especially if you're pregnant, breastfeeding or on medication.

## Your Cycle & the Scale

If you menstruate, your body weight naturally fluctuates during your cycle:

- **Luteal phase (the week or so before your period):** water retention can add 2–5 lb, appetite often increases and training may feel harder.
- **During/after your period:** water weight drops, often suddenly.

### What to do

- Compare your weight to the **same point in your previous cycle**, not last week.
- Expect cravings before your period. Plan a slightly larger, high-protein meal or a planned treat rather than fighting it.
- If you feel weaker, train at RIR 3 and skip PR attempts.

::quiz
Q: Your weight jumps 3 lb in the week before your period. What's the most likely cause?
- Fat gain
* Water retention
- Muscle loss
E: Hormonal shifts in the luteal phase commonly cause water retention, which drops after your period starts.
::

## Breaking a Fat-Loss Plateau

Before you change anything, make sure it's a real plateau: **no change in your weekly average weight AND measurements for 3+ weeks** (and you're not just before your period).

### The checklist

1. **Are you tracking accurately?** Weigh food for a week. Oils, sauces, drinks and bites add up quickly.
2. **Are your weekends undoing your week?**
3. **Have your steps dropped?** NEAT often decreases unconsciously when you diet.
4. **Is your sleep under 7 hours?**
5. **How long have you been dieting?** After 8–12 weeks in a deficit, a 1–2 week **diet break at maintenance** can help you reset mentally and physically.

### Then adjust (choose one)

- Reduce calories by 100–150, or
- Add 2,000 daily steps.

## The 90-Minute Meal Prep System

Meal prep is the habit that makes everything else easier. When healthy food is ready, you eat it. When it isn't, you order takeout.

### The system

1. **Plan (10 min, Saturday):** choose 2 proteins, 2 carbs and 2–3 veggies for the week. Write your grocery list using the Grocery tab.
2. **Shop (Saturday or Sunday).**
3. **Prep (90 min, Sunday):** follow the Sunday Prep checklist. Oven, stovetop and rice cooker all run at the same time.
4. **Mid-week mini prep (20 min, Wednesday):** cook one more batch of protein so food stays fresh.

### Food safety

- Cooked food keeps 3–4 days in the fridge.
- Freeze anything for Thursday–Saturday on Sunday and thaw it the night before.
- Cool food within 2 hours of cooking before refrigerating.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I have to count calories forever?**
No. Track for 4–8 weeks to learn portion sizes, then switch to the plate method. Return to tracking when progress stalls.

**Can I eat at night?**
Yes. Meal timing doesn't override total calories. If eating at night helps you stick to your plan, do it.

**Is fruit too high in sugar?**
No. Whole fruit comes with fiber, water and vitamins. It's one of the best carb sources you can eat.

**I'm vegetarian. Can I still hit my protein?**
Yes: Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, eggs, tofu, tempeh, seitan, edamame, lentils and protein powder. Combine several sources every day.

**What if I'm not hungry enough to eat all my food?**
Use more calorie-dense foods (nut butters, olive oil, granola, dried fruit), drink some calories (smoothies) and eat more frequently.
